Why intuition fails

ASSUMPTION ≠ VISUAL FACT

▚ THE COMFORTABLE ANSWER

For two independent doors that each open with probability ½, the chance that at least one opens is ¾. Familiar, tidy — and completely reliant on an assumption the picture does not prove.

1 − (½)² = 75%VALID ONLY IF THE DOORS ARE INDEPENDENT

▚ WHAT THE MAZE ACTUALLY ENCODES

Under this maze's ideal corridor constraints, the two bridge doors must agree — they open and close together. Exhaustive classical proof checked all 65,536 sixteen-bit worlds: four satisfy the ideal constraints, and none has exactly one open bridge.

P(escape) = 50% · but only with a balanced bridge correlationTHE EQUALITY THEOREM ALONE DOES NOT PROVE THAT PROBABILITY

What is quantum here?
HONEST LIMITATIONS

    

      
The completed Atlas graph-v1 job used mode emu, backend aer —
      quantum-circuit emulation on a classical simulator, no physical QPU. The returned
      distribution is real Atlas job evidence; the finite proof and path certificates are classical.
      This demo does not claim quantum advantage or experimental confirmation
      of the balanced 50% target.

      
What survives the honesty cut: a graph engine asked to prepare a 16-qubit state whose
      measured edges match requested Pauli expectations, a real job lifecycle with saved
      request / status / raw JSON, and a lesson in dependence — the same reason quantum
      correlation is not just "two independent chances" either. That is the real teaching core:
      probability you can see.
